Why most mice are capped

How brands limit your CPS

Butterfly, jitter and drag clicking all hit one button many times, very fast. Whether those presses count depends on the mouse, not just your fingers.

Debounce: the built-in delay

A mechanical switch “bounces” for a few milliseconds when pressed: its metal contacts touch and separate several times. So the mouse’s firmware waits a set time after each click (the debounce) and ignores anything inside it. A drag or butterfly click puts presses only a few milliseconds apart, so they land inside that window and get merged into one click. That window is the cap.

Why brands set it high

As switches wear, they start double-clicking by themselves, which is the most common reason mice get returned. A longer debounce hides that, so most brands ship a fixed, safe delay and don’t let you change it. Great for reliability, bad for CPS.

Optical switches never bounce

Optical and hybrid switches (most Razer mice, Logitech’s LIGHTFORCE) read the press with a beam of light, so there’s no bounce at all: one press is always exactly one click. Brilliant for FPS, but a butterfly or drag click can never sneak in an extra one.

What “double-clicks” means

A mouse that double-clicks has a short enough debounce (Glorious lets you set it near 0 ms) that one quick finger movement can register as two clicks. That is how butterfly clicking reaches 20+ CPS. Some servers limit CPS or ban double-click mice and macros, so check the rules where you play.

Why we only list verified mice

Plenty of forum posts claim a mouse “can drag click”. ProMouser only marks a mouse as double-clicking once it has been checked by a person. Every other mouse is listed as can’t double-click until it is, even if it has an adjustable debounce on paper. Right now that verified list is the Glorious Model O, Bloody A70, Glorious Model O-, Glorious Model D, HyperX Pulsefire Haste 2, Attack Shark X3 and Ant Esports GM320.

Quick setup tips

Use the right mouse for the way you play

Palm grip

Best if you want relaxed control, especially for long mining or building sessions.

Claw / fingertip grip

Good if you prefer quick camera movement, fast flicks, and a slightly lighter feel.

Buttons & bindings

Use extra buttons for sprint, inventory shortcuts, or quick utility actions to reduce hand strain.
